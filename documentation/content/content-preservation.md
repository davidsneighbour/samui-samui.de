# Content preservation

Published content is part of the historical record of samui-samui.de. Preservation takes precedence over removal.

* Agents MUST NOT delete published posts merely because content is outdated, incomplete, unavailable, or no longer relevant.
* Missing external media, broken links, failed availability checks, and relic status MUST NOT be interpreted as deletion authorisation.
* Agents MUST prefer restoration, contextual annotation, or an approved reversible relic classification.
* Automated checks MUST NOT classify posts as relics or change editorial reviews.
* Agents MUST preserve original publication dates, permalinks, surviving content, media identifiers, and historical metadata.

## Mandatory double opt-in for permanent deletion

Permanent deletion of any published post requires **two separate, explicit approvals from the repository owner**.

1. Present the proposed deletion, identifying every affected post by title, permalink, and source file. Explain why restoration or relic classification is unsuitable. Obtain the first explicit approval.
2. After that first approval, present the final deletion scope and request a second explicit confirmation. Do not modify or remove any affected files before that second approval arrives.

The initial request to investigate or delete content does not substitute for either approval. Silence, approval of a general implementation plan, batch instructions, automated reports, and classification as a relic do not count as approval. Without both approvals, preserve the content.

This rule also applies to bulk operations, migrations, maintenance scripts, refactoring, and repository clean-up. Removing a source while retaining a generated page, removing its route, or replacing its original content with an empty stub also requires these approvals. Tools and agents MUST NOT bypass the policy by describing permanent deletion as archival or relic classification.
