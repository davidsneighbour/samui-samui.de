# Research guidelines

## Search beyond the obvious English spelling

Koh Samui may appear as:

* Koh Samui
* Ko Samui
* Samui
* เกาะสมุย
* สมุย

Search all relevant forms.

## Publication date is not event date

An article published during the research period may describe:

* an older event;
* an upcoming event;
* a repeated press release;
* a newly released report concerning an earlier period.

Record both dates wherever possible.

## Citation snippets

Use named Markdown footnotes for source citations, following
`documentation/source-citations.md`.

Do not use numeric source footnotes such as `[^1]`. Use semantic identifiers:

```text
[^src-<source-slug>-<yyyymmdd>-<topic-slug>]
```

For report items that may be copied into a post, keep the inline marker and the
matching footnote definition close together under `Kopierbare Quellen-Fussnoten`.
The goal is that Patrick can copy a paragraph or story block without losing its
source citation.

In the footnote definition, link the source item title by default. Use a raw URL
only when the title is unavailable, unstable, or the exact URL is editorially
relevant.

## Relative periods and time zones

When no period is specified, research the past 10 days: yesterday plus the nine
calendar days before yesterday. Exclude today unless the user explicitly asks
for today.

When the user asks for "last week", use yesterday as the end date and include
the seven calendar days before yesterday. Exclude today unless the user
explicitly asks for today, because same-day news pages can be incomplete,
reordered, or corrected during the day.

When the user asks for "this month", use the current calendar month as the
bracket: the first day of the current month through the last day of the current
month. If the month is still in progress, include only published material up to
the latest reliable available date and state that the month is incomplete.

When the user asks for "last month", use the full previous calendar month as the
bracket: the first day through the last day of that month.

For international sources, do not reject an otherwise relevant story merely
because the publication timestamp falls just outside the requested window in one
timezone. Compare the source-local date, the Thailand date, and nearby
international date boundaries. Include the story when that reasonable timezone
reading places it in range, and mark the date ambiguity in the source notes.

## Thai sources should not be treated as secondary by default

Thai municipal, regional, government, and local news sources may be the primary reporting.

International reporting frequently republishes or simplifies Thai coverage.

Find the original Thai report where possible.

## Circles and relevance weights

Classify every included story into one circle:

* `Samui` for Koh Samui and directly connected Gulf-of-Thailand local stories;
* `Thailand` for national or Thailand-wide stories relevant to the blog;
* `Asia/Region` for wider regional stories only when they plausibly matter to
  Thailand, travel, Germans abroad, public health, regional politics, or culture.

Assign every story an A/B/C relevance weight:

* `A` means strong `samui-samui.de` candidate;
* `B` means useful context or possible article with more work;
* `C` means watchlist/background signal.

Be opinionated about what German readers may care about: visas, bureaucracy,
safety, tourism, living abroad, retirees, families, cultural friction,
monarchy/public rituals, people coming to Thailand, entertainment, travel
routes, odd local stories, and German-media framing. Do not include general tech
topics unless the Thailand-life angle is the story.

## Translation

Translate meaning, not just words.

Preserve names of organisations, locations, projects, festivals, and legal bodies accurately.

When a translation is uncertain, include the original Thai wording.

## Tourism awards

Tourism awards require additional scrutiny.

Check:

* who organised the award;
* how winners were chosen;
* whether the result was reader-voted, editorial, commercial, or sponsored;
* whether the same announcement is being republished by tourism organisations;
* whether the award creates a meaningful local story.

## Crime and legal reporting

Avoid sensationalism.

Distinguish:

* allegation;
* arrest;
* raid;
* charge;
* trial;
* judgment;
* administrative action.

Do not describe allegations as established facts.

## Announced events

For future events, verify:

* organiser;
* exact date;
* location;
* official page;
* cancellation or postponement notices;
* whether public attendance is actually possible.

## Suggested blog topics

A usable blog topic must contain more than a rewritten headline.

It should support:

* personal context;
* local observation;
* historical comparison;
* practical advice;
* analysis;
* criticism;
* humour;
* follow-up reporting.
